Select a clip and two small square grips appear on its top corners. Drag one
inward and it draws a fade: the clip comes up from nothing at the start, or goes
away to nothing at the end.

Every clip can fade, not only audio. What "fade" means depends on the clip:
picture fades to nothing, sound fades to silence.

## Draw a fade

Grab the small square on a top corner and drag it towards the middle of the
clip. The wedge follows as you drag, and the length is written when you let go.

<Frame caption="Dragging the fade-in grip, then the fade-out grip.">
  <video

Drag a grip back to its corner to remove the fade. The whole gesture is one
history entry, so a single undo takes the fade away.

Two fades on one clip cannot overlap. Drag both past the middle and they share
the clip in proportion rather than fighting over it, which is also how the
runtime plays a clip trimmed shorter than its own fades.

## Bend the curve

A straight fade is not always the one you want. Drag the dot sitting on the fade
line to bend it, and the curve follows your pointer.

<Frame caption="The same fade, dragged down and then up. The dot stays under the pointer because the curve is drawn through it.">
  <video
    className="aspect-video w-full object-cover"
    src="https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/docs/images/studio/fade-bend-v1.mp4#t=0.1"
    poster="https://static.heygen.ai/hyperframes-oss/docs/images/studio/fade-bend-v1.jpg"
    controls
    muted
    playsInline
    loop

- **Drag down** and the fade starts slowly and finishes fast. It holds near
  nothing, then arrives late. Useful under a voice, where you want the music out
  of the way early.
- **Drag up** and the fade starts fast and finishes slowly. It gets most of the
  way there immediately, then eases in. The least noticeable fade.
- **Drag back through the middle** and it is exactly straight again.

Each fade has its own dot and its own shape. Bending the one at the start leaves
the one at the end exactly as it was.

The dot only appears once there is a fade to bend. On a clip with none there is
no line to pull, so start with the corner grips.

## What it writes

A fade is stored where that kind of clip already keeps that kind of information,
so it stays editable afterwards by whatever already edits it.

### Picture

Visual clips carry three attributes the runtime reads. Write them by hand and
they work with no Studio involved:

```html
<div id="title" class="clip" data-start="0" data-duration="8"
     data-fade-in="1.5" data-fade-out="1.2" data-fade-curve="-0.5">
  Hello
</div>
```

| Attribute | Value | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `data-fade-in` | Seconds of fade at the clip's head | 0 |
| `data-fade-out` | Seconds of fade at the clip's tail | 0 |
| `data-fade-curve` | How far each ramp bends, from -1 to 1 | 0 |

`data-fade-curve` is a number rather than a named shape because the shape is
something you drag, and a drag has to be able to land anywhere in between. Zero
is a straight ramp. Negative sags the line, so the fade starts slow and finishes
fast. Positive bulges it the other way.

It takes one value or two, the way `border-radius` does. One value bends both
ramps alike; two bend the head and then the tail, so a fade in that creeps out
of black can sit on the same clip as a fade out that drops away:

```html
<div id="title" class="clip" data-start="0" data-duration="8"
     data-fade-in="1.5" data-fade-out="1.2" data-fade-curve="-0.87 1">
  Hello
</div>
```

<Note>
  The fade is applied as a CSS `filter`, not as `opacity`. Opacity is the
  property animation engines drive, so a runtime writing it every frame would
  fight your tweens for it. A filter multiplies with whatever they set, and it
  composes onto whatever filter you wrote yourself.
</Note>

### Sound

Audio clips store a fade as the first and last segment of their volume envelope,
not as an attribute. That keeps it editable as breakpoints: expand the clip's
automation lane afterwards and the fade is there as points you can drag, add to,
or reshape.

```html
<audio id="bed" class="clip" src="assets/bed.m4a" data-start="0" data-duration="16"
       data-automation='{"version":1,"lanes":[{"target":"volume",
                         "points":[{"t":0,"v":0},{"t":2,"v":1}]}]}'></audio>
```

Two breakpoints: silent at 0s, full at 2s. The grip and the lane are two views of
the same thing, so a fade drawn with one shows up in the other.

Both media draw and play the same curve, so a bend of -0.5 looks the same on a
music clip as on a video clip.

## Fades survive editing

A fade is declared, not animated: it says how long it lasts, not which frames it
covers. Trim the clip, move it, or slip it and the fade stays attached to the
clip's own start and end rather than to a moment on the timeline. There is no
tween to keep in sync.
