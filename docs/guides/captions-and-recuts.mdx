## Make it

Add the clip in **Make something** and say what you want:

```text
Add clear animated captions to this interview. Keep the footage and its spoken edit unchanged.
```

**Like the captions in someone else's video?** Paste its link too and say "captions like this". With your own agent instead of the app, start the message with `/hyperframes`.

## Ask for one of three things

- **Captions:** the spoken words on screen, timed to the voice. A clean line under the speaker for most videos, or
  big words set behind the speaker for a cinematic look.
- **Designed cards:** titles, pull-quotes, number callouts, lower thirds, or the speaker shrunk into a corner while a
  card holds, all synced to what's said.
- **A tighter cut:** remove long pauses or stretches you name. Ask for it first, then caption the cut, so the
  captions follow the final timing:

```text
cut the pauses longer than 0.8 s out of assets/interview.mp4, then add clean
captions to the tighter cut.
```

To reorder the story or pick the best moments from a long recording, describe the video you want as a
[custom video](/guides/general-video).

Tell the agent any names or terms the transcript may miss, and where the speaker's face and any on-screen text are,
so graphics stay clear of them.

## Prompts that work

Two words help: the **rail** is the line of captions that carries the spoken words; an **embed** is one big word set
behind the speaker for the strongest moment.

> Add captions to ./interview.mp4. Clean verbatim rail carrying the spoken words, readable lower-third. Promote the single hardest-hitting word to an embed behind the speaker; highlight one key word in each rail line. Keep the source aspect ratio. Footage stays untouched.

The rail carries most of the text; an **embed** is the scarce, earned peak — one big word matted behind the subject at the climax, never every line. Embedding the whole transcript is the most common mistake.

### Designed cards

> Package ./founder-clip.mp4 with designed graphic overlay cards synced to the transcript. 9:16 portrait, warm-paper style. Open with a fullscreen kicker + title hook, drop a lower-third when she names the company, a data callout card counting up the "200+ teams / $1.2M ARR" stat, and a pull-quote card for the strongest line. Speaker stays full-bleed under the cards; shrink her into a corner PiP while the data card holds. The clip plays untouched underneath.

You describe the *cards* — their content, timing, and how the speaker shares the canvas with them (full-bleed, split, PiP, or glass overlay). The agent designs and writes each card; there's no fixed archetype list, so the overlays follow what the transcript actually says.

### Say these when they matter

**Look and tone (captions).** Describe the look by content: explainer / interview / must-read words → a clean readable rail; poetic / social / cinematic → hero typography behind the speaker; hype → bolder, punchier type. Unsure → a clean rail: the words read and the scene stays safe. Ready-made caption styles are in the [captions catalog](/prompting/captions-catalog).

**Verbatim rail vs climax embed.** The rail is the default and carries most of the text. An embed is a promotion — one peak word matted behind the subject, scarce and spaced (roughly one per beat, never two co-visible, at most one apex). Tell the agent *which* lines earn the embed; leave the rest on the rail.

**Style, layout, and canvas (overlay cards).** Name a style (warm paper, clinical, …), a layout (split / stack / PiP / overlay), and a canvas ratio. Matching the source ratio is the safe default, but you choose — 16:9 for desktop / YouTube, 9:16 for Reels / Shorts, 4:5 for feed.

**Keyword highlighting.** On the caption rail, a punch word can carry an inline `emphasis` — an accent-color or active-word pop — without leaving the rail. Ask for "highlight the key word in each line" and it stays readable.

### Common mistakes

**Captioning before cutting.** Captions are timed to the clip as it is. Cut first, then caption, or the captions
drift off the words.
- **Avoid:** `add captions, then remove the pauses`
- **Instead:** `remove the pauses, then add captions to the cut`

**Embedding every word.** On a talking head the rail is the verbatim default; matting every caption behind the subject buries the words and spends the climax on nothing.
- **Avoid:** `composite every caption behind the speaker for a cinematic look` (on an explainer)
- **Instead:** `verbatim rail; promote only the two payoff lines to an embed`

**Multi-subject clips.** Text behind the speaker is matted against one person; two speakers or hard cuts make it flicker.
- **Avoid:** `caption this two-person podcast in one pass`
- **Instead:** `split the clip per shot / per speaker first, then caption each` — or use a single-subject cut.

## Review what matters

- Names, product terms, and numbers are correct.
- Captions follow the spoken words.
- Graphics avoid faces and important parts of the footage.
- The video works with sound and muted.
- The final caption or graphic clears before the video ends.

Then [change it](/studio-app/edit-with-the-agent): ask the agent for corrected words or timing, or move a caption by hand
in the editor.

To import corrected words from a transcript file, run `hyperframes transcribe transcript.json --dir ./my-video`.
It replaces the full transcript in the existing captions and keeps the caption code around it.
