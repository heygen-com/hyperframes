## Make it

**Start from an explainer you like.** In the Studio app, open **Reference** on Home and paste its link (YouTube,
TikTok, Instagram or X), upload the file, or choose one under **Pick a reference**. Then say what yours is about: you
get that pacing and drawing style, about your topic. You don't have to love all of it; one thing you like is
enough to start from. Reference uses your HeyGen API key, and the app asks for it the first time.

**Or describe it** in **Make something**, with your notes attached or pasted:

```text
Turn these notes into a 60-second explainer for people new to the topic: [paste notes]
```

Say which facts or wording must stay exact. With your own agent instead of the app, start the message with `/hyperframes`.

## How should the idea land?

- **Understand one idea** — open with a useful question, then reveal the mechanism one layer at a time.
- **Follow a process** — keep one visual stage and show each step in the order the viewer needs it.
- **Remember three points** — make the ideas parallel, concrete, and visually distinct enough to recall later.
- **Learn through a story** — use one case to move from setup and tension to a general lesson.

If you supply an approved script, say whether HyperFrames may restructure it or
must keep every word.

Use a [product and website video](/guides/product-launch-video) when the real interface should provide the proof. Use [captions or designed overlays](/guides/captions-and-recuts) when existing footage should remain underneath.

## From information to understanding

HyperFrames does not turn each paragraph into a scene. It finds the teaching spine, removes asides, and builds a sequence around the one idea the viewer should retain.

The visuals share one coherent language and develop with the explanation. A diagram should reveal a mechanism; a chart should prove a claim; motion should show what changes.

### Say these when they matter

You can steer all of these from the prompt, before you have learned any
technique.

| Knob | What to say | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Verbatim vs summarized** | "use my wording verbatim" or "restructure it freely" | Verbatim keeps your voice but locks the word count. Summarized lets it cut and reorder for pace. |
| **Duration** | "~60 seconds", never "60 seconds" | With a script, the narration sets the real length. A hard number forces the agent to trim or pad the words. |
| **Scene density** | "one idea per scene" | A faceless scene has one invented focal to animate. Two ideas leave nothing to build the motion around. It reads as a text dump. |
| **Angle** | "concept" / "how-to" / "listicle" / "story" | The angle decides the story shape. The agent reshapes your text into it instead of reading your paragraphs in order. |
| **Caption style** | "embedded captions, keywords highlighted in the accent color" | Captions are burned in. Naming the highlight color ties them to your palette instead of a default pill. |
| **Palette** | "brand color #FF5533 on off-black" | There is no site to borrow from, so the agent picks a palette. A named accent and ground personalize it. |
| **Voice** | "male TTS voice, calm" / "warm female voice" | Gender and tone are prompt words. The provider is the agent's decision. |

<Tip>
  Scene density is the single biggest quality lever here. "One idea per scene"
  turns a dense paragraph into a paced sequence. The agent reorders and
  compresses your text to hit it. That is what makes an explainer teach instead
  of recite.
</Tip>

### Common mistakes

**"60 seconds" instead of "~60 seconds".** Same rule as on the
[product launch page](/guides/product-launch-video#prompts-that-work). It bites
harder here, because the script is the whole video. You cannot know a supplied
script's spoken duration until the TTS renders.

- **Avoid:** `a 60-second explainer from this text: ...`
- **Instead:** `a ~60-second explainer from this text: ...`

**Cramming ideas into a scene.** Every faceless visual is invented around a
single focal. Overload the scene and there is no clear thing to animate.

- **Avoid:** `explain all five caching layers in one scene`
- **Instead:** `one idea per scene — one caching layer at a time`

**Asking it to capture or pull real imagery.** There is no capture step. A
faceless explainer invents its visuals.

- **Avoid:** `pull screenshots from the site and explain the feature`
- **Instead:** that's a site or product video — give it the URL
  ([product launch](/guides/product-launch-video#prompts-that-work))

**Leaving the look unspecified when you care.** There is no brand to read, so
the agent picks the palette. If you have colors, name them.

- **Avoid:** `make it look on-brand`
- **Instead:** `brand color #FF5533 on off-black`


## Review whether it teaches

- The opening creates a question, tension, or surprise.
- The main idea is clear by the second scene.
- Each body scene advances one mechanism, step, example, or implication.
- Numbers and factual claims match the source.
- The ending lands the sentence you wanted the viewer to remember.

Ask the agent when the teaching sequence or evidence must change. Use
the browser editor for a precise visual, timing, or animation correction.
