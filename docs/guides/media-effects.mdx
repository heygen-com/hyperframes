An effect changes how a picture looks, not what it is. The file stays untouched, so you can soften the effect,
animate it or take it off later.

## Ask for a look

Say what it should feel like; you don't need the effect names:

```text
Make the product clip feel like a clean two-ink editorial print. Keep the label readable.
The interview looks too dark and slightly cold. Keep skin natural, no filtered look.
Pixelate the email address in the screen recording from 0:04 to 0:07.
```

Works the same in the Studio app and with your own agent. To copy a look from a video you like, add it as a
reference and say "grade it like this".

## The effects

- **Essentials:** Blur, Pixelate, Bloom. Hide something, pull focus, make highlights glow.
- **Retro and glitch:** Tape Damage, Film Artifacts, Scanlines, CRT Curvature, Channel Separation, Digital Glitch,
  Chroma Softening. Old tape, old film, old monitors, broken signals, with real tracking wobble and color bleed.
- **Print:** Halftone, Two-Ink Print, Ordered Dither, Mono Screen.
- **Art:** ASCII (eight styles), Engraving, Crosshatch, Kuwahara Paint.

Commit to a strong look or leave it off: a half-strength ASCII reads as a mistake. For grain and vignette, use the
color controls below; for HUDs, light leaks and flashes, use a [Catalog overlay](/catalog).

## Fix and grade the color

Correct first (exposure, white balance, contrast, saturation), then style. No look rescues blown highlights or the
wrong shot.

## Adjust it by hand

In the [browser editor](/studio/index), select an image or video and open **Effects** or **Grade** in the Inspector.
**Grade** starts with thirteen presets, each previewed on your own frame, and a **Strength** slider. Hold the compare
button to flash back to the original, and scrub a few places in moving footage: a grade that flatters one frame can
ruin the next.

| Control | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Preset and strength | Pick a whole look, then dial it back |
| Exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, whites, blacks | Brightness and contrast, overall and per tonal range |
| Warmth, tint, vibrance, saturation | Take out a color cast, or push color further |
| Color wheels | Tint shadows, midtones and highlights separately |
| RGB curves | Redraw the brightness ramp — whole image, or red, green and blue one at a time |
| Hue curves | Pick one hue and move only it: shift, saturate or brighten |
| HSL selections | Key a band of hue, saturation and brightness, then correct only those pixels. Up to four, in order |
| Grain and vignette | Add texture, or darken the edges to pull the eye in |
| Custom LUT | Load a `.cube` file that maps every color to another |

The scopes measure the picture so you aren't judging by your screen: the **histogram** shows lost detail at either
end, the **waveform** shows where across the frame, the **RGB parade** names a color cast, and the **vectorscope**
puts skin tones on one known line.

**Copy grade to** reuses a grade on other clips, as a starting point; the same numbers rarely suit two shots.

## LUTs

A LUT (a `.cube` file, up to 64 points per side) maps every color to another, blended in at the strength you set.
Use creative Rec.709 LUTs. HyperFrames doesn't convert camera LOG footage first, so a LOG clip needs its camera's own
transform or comes out wrong.

## Limits

- An effect or grade covers the whole layer. It doesn't follow a face or an object: to treat part of the frame, put
  that part on its own layer.
- It applies to images, video and canvas, not to text or HTML.
- Standard video only. HDR sources preview in SDR and the effects don't carry into true HDR.
- Blur, Bloom and Kuwahara Paint are slow; several stacked on big layers slow preview and render.
- Keep media inside the project. A file on another server needs the right CORS headers and can vanish before render.

<Accordion title="For agents and code: the commands and the stored format">

List what exists, measure a source, then apply. `--dry-run` shows the change, `--clear` removes it.

```bash
npx hyperframes media-treatment --capabilities --json
npx hyperframes media-treatment --selector '#interview' --analyze --json
npx hyperframes media-treatment --selector '#product' --grading '{"preset":"two-ink-print"}' --apply --json
```

Compare several looks on one frame (up to sixteen, the original first):

```bash
npx hyperframes grade-compare --for frame.png --grades grades.json
```

Phone photos keep their displayed orientation and aspect ratio in the sheet; the source image stays unchanged.

Everything lands in `data-color-grading`, in named sections: `adjust` for corrections, `details` for grain and
vignette, `effects` for treatments. A flat object such as `{"exposure":0.15}` renders nothing, and
`npx hyperframes lint` says which section it belongs in.

```html
<video src="./interview.mp4" data-color-grading='{"preset":"skin-soft","intensity":0.7,"adjust":{"exposure":0.15},"effects":{"bloom":0.4}}'></video>
```

The full format is in the [HTML schema](/reference/html-schema#color-grading-and-media-effects) and the
[color grading reference](/reference/color-grading).
